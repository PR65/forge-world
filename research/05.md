# Industry Decomposition Sources: Comprehensive Research Note for FORGE WORLD

## 1. Scope

This research note examines the structural data sources, methodologies, and analytical frameworks required for **industry decomposition** across major hard-tech sectors (such as semiconductors, aerospace, advanced energy systems, electric vehicles, and heavy manufacturing). Specifically, this stream investigates **Bills of Materials (BOMs)**, **product and assembly teardowns**, **process databases**, **life-cycle inventory (LCI)**, **customs and supply-chain trade relationships**, **engineering datasets**, **domain ontologies**, **knowledge graphs**, **patent corpuses**, **procurement catalogs**, **technical standards**, and **maintenance/technical manuals**. 

The objective is to establish an authoritative catalog and evaluation of primary data sources, quantitative metrics, systemic relationships, and data gaps to inform a future physical-industry planning and simulation system (Forge World). The geographic scope is global (with emphasis on North America, Europe, East Asia, and international trade corridors), and the temporal scope covers foundational historical baselines up to current 2026 industrial data architectures.

## 2. Executive findings

Industry decomposition requires synthesizing heterogeneous micro-level engineering specifications with macro-level trade and process flows. Key findings across the twelve investigated source categories include:

* **BOMs & Teardown Analytics**: While proprietary enterprise BOMs (Engineering BOMs [EBOMs], Manufacturing BOMs [MBOMs], Service BOMs [SBOMs]) remain siloed inside ERP/PLM systems (SAP, Oracle, Teamcenter), professional teardown services (e.g., Techinsights, Munro & Associates, iFixit) provide empirical, component-level hardware breakdowns. These sources yield precise geometric, material, and cost-to-feature estimates, but suffer from high commercial costs and point-in-time snapshot limitations [1].
* **Process Databases & Life-Cycle Inventories (LCI)**: Global process datasets such as **ecoinvent** (housing >18,000 unit processes) [2], the **U.S. Life Cycle Inventory (USLCI)** [3], and the **European Reference Life Cycle Data System (ELCD)** provide rigorous stoichiometric, energy, and emissions inputs per unit of industrial output. However, their geographic resolution and updating cadence often lag rapid technological shifts in emerging hard-tech domains (e.g., advanced battery chemistries, compound semiconductors).
* **Customs Trade & Supply Chain Networks**: Macro-level structural dependencies are mapped via trade databases like **UN Comtrade** [4], **ImportGenius**, and **Panjiva**. These customs declarations link Harmonized System (HS) codes to specific shipping entities, uncovering multi-tier supplier relationships, cross-border material flows, and geographic bottlenecks, albeit obscured by trade secrecy, intermediary shell entities, and aggregated HS code categories.
* **Knowledge Graphs & Ontologies**: Industrial ontologies (such as **BFO [Basic Formal Ontology]**, **ISO 15926** for process plants, and custom semantic web graphs) enable the logical integration of disparate engineering datasets. By linking parts, processes, material properties, and standards through explicit semantic relations, these frameworks provide the ontological spine necessary for automated physical-industry simulation.
* **Standards & Technical Manuals**: Engineering standards (ISO, ASTM, IEEE, SAE) and OEM technical manuals codify allowable tolerances, material grades, testing procedures, and assembly instructions. They serve as deterministic rule engines for physical manufacturing feasibility but lack machine-readable, graph-native interfaces across disparate publishing bodies.

## 4. Source and dataset inventory

To operationalize industry decomposition for Forge World, the following inventory of primary data sources, access interfaces, and data structures must be integrated:

1. **Process & LCI Inventories**:
   * **ecoinvent**: Global LCI database. Access via ecoinvent direct portal or openLCA Nexus. Coverage: Global, multi-sector (energy, chemicals, metals, electronics). License: Commercial/Institutional subscription.
   * **USLCI**: U.S. Federal and NREL-curated LCI data. Access via GitHub / openLCA Nexus. Coverage: United States, industrial commodities. License: Public Domain / Open Source.
2. **Trade & Economic Flow Datasets**:
   * **UN Comtrade Plus**: Global merchandise trade statistics. Access via REST API (up to 100k records per call) and web portal. Coverage: 193 countries, 1962–present. License: Open Data / Free tier with registration.
   * **OECD Structural Analysis (STAN) Database**: Industrial economic activity, R&D, and trade metrics. Access via OECD iLibrary / API. Coverage: OECD member states and key partners. License: Open terms with attribution.
3. **Intellectual Property & Standards**:
   * **USPTO / EPO Espacenet**: Global patent filings. Access via bulk data archives (PatentsView) and APIs. Coverage: Worldwide patent literature. License: Public domain data.
   * **Standards Organizations (ISO, ASTM, IEEE)**: Engineering specifications. Access via institutional subscriptions and web stores. License: Proprietary / Paid standards.
4. **Hardware & Teardown Intelligence**:
   * **iFixit Open Repair Data**: Open-source repair manuals, teardown guides, and tool listings. Access via web and API. Coverage: Consumer electronics, appliances. License: Creative Commons / Open.
   * **Commercial Teardown Providers (Techinsights, Munro)**: Deep engineering analysis. Access via enterprise subscription. Coverage: Semiconductors, EVs, advanced electronics. License: Proprietary commercial.

## 5. Relationships/variables useful for Forge World

A physical-industry planning system requires computable mappings between abstract industrial requirements and concrete physical realities. The key mathematical and logical relationships identified in this stream include:

* **Bill of Materials Explosion & Matrix Multiplication**:
  $$\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{P}$$
  Where $\mathbf{B}$ is the multi-level BOM matrix (defining quantity of sub-components per assembly), $\mathbf{P}$ is the production schedule vector for final goods, and $\mathbf{Q}$ is the total gross requirement vector for raw materials and intermediate parts.
* **Life Cycle Inventory Input-Output Balancing**:
  $$\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} \cdot \mathbf{f}$$
  Where $\mathbf{A}$ is the technology matrix of unit processes (describing inputs and outputs of industrial activities), $\mathbf{f}$ is the final demand vector, and $\mathbf{g}$ is the scaling vector determining total industrial activity levels required to satisfy demand while accounting for environmental emissions and resource depletion.
* **Customs-to-Supply-Chain Graph Edges**:
  Graph edges $E(u, v)$ connecting industrial entities $u$ and $v$ weighted by shipment frequency, HS code volume, and geographic transit times, enabling bottleneck identification and single-point-of-failure analysis across hard-tech supply chains.

## 6. Gaps and unknowns

* **Proprietary Granularity Wall**: Detailed MBOMs, internal supplier tiering, and proprietary yield rates for advanced nodes (e.g., sub-2nm semiconductors, advanced aerospace composites) are closely guarded trade secrets, forcing reliance on reverse-engineered estimates.
* **Temporal Lag in LCI Datasets**: LCI databases often operate on multi-year release cycles, making them structurally blind to rapid carbon-intensity shifts or novel recycling loops in emerging hard-tech domains.
* **Ontological Fragmentation**: While standards like ISO 15926, BFO, and domain-specific schemas exist, there is no universal, unified ontology that seamlessly maps atomic physical material properties, dynamic manufacturing process constraints, and macroeconomic trade flows into a single queryable graph.
