# Research Note: Global Capability Analogues for Forge World

## Scope

This research note investigates global capability analogues across twelve major analytical frameworks and institutional repositories, specifically focusing on the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) Innovation Capabilities Navigator and Global Innovation Index (GII) [1] [2], the Harvard Growth Lab's Atlas of Economic Complexity and Product Space [3] [4], the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) STAN and Trade in Value Added (TiVA) databases [5] [6], the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO) Competitive Industrial Performance (CIP) Index [7] [8], the World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) and Enterprise Surveys [9] [10], national defence-industrial mapping and European strategic dependencies (e.g., Critical Raw Materials Act and JRM/RMIS) [11] [12], and Asian industrial capability studies (such as Japan's METI White Papers) [13] [14]. 

The primary objective is to evaluate how these international frameworks measure, model, and project industrial and technological capabilities, supply chain dependencies, and economic complexity. This knowledge directly informs the architectural requirements of a future physical-industry planning system (**Forge World**), which requires robust quantitative metrics, network topologies, and capability maturity models for real-world manufacturing and resource allocation.

---

## Executive Findings

Global capability measurement has shifted from static macroeconomic indicators (such as aggregate GDP or manufacturing share of value added) to granular, network-based, and big-data-driven ecosystem tracking. Institutional frameworks now map millions of discrete data points across patents, trademarks, scientific publications, international trade, and firm-level surveys to assess national preparedness and vulnerability.

> "The Innovation Capabilities Navigator maps innovation ecosystems using big data from patents, trademarks, publications, and exports, covering 193 economies and over 2,500 innovation capabilities across science and technology [1] [2]."

Key structural findings across the evaluated streams include:
1. **Granular Ecosystem Mapping (WIPO)**: WIPO's newly introduced *Innovation Capabilities Navigator* (launched in mid-2026) bridges macroeconomic indices and micro-level patent/publication flows, tracking over 2,500 distinct capability vectors across 193 economies [1] [2]. This provides a high-dimensional analogue for capability vector spaces in Forge World.
2. **Economic Complexity and the Product Space (Harvard Growth Lab)**: The Atlas of Economic Complexity demonstrates that a nation's export basket reflects its underlying tacit capabilities. Countries do not grow by merely producing more of the same goods, but by acquiring complex "recipes" and moving into denser regions of the Product Space [3] [4].
3. **Value-Added Interconnectedness (OECD TiVA & STAN)**: Traditional gross trade statistics obscure domestic versus foreign value-added contributions. OECD TiVA (2025 edition) maps global value chains (GVCs) across 80 economies, revealing deep cross-border interdependencies in advanced manufacturing and intermediate inputs [5] [6].
4. **Industrial Competitiveness Benchmarking (UNIDO CIP)**: UNIDO's revamped Competitive Industrial Performance Index evaluates countries on their capacity to produce and export manufactured goods, technological deepening, and global industrial impact, serving as a baseline for physical output benchmarking [7] [8].
5. **Strategic Dependencies and Defence Mapping**: European initiatives, such as the Critical Raw Materials Act and the Joint Research Centre's Raw Materials Information System (RMIS), systematically map material supply bottlenecks, Single Points of Failure (SPOFs), and defence-industrial vulnerabilities [11] [12].

## Relationships/variables Useful for Forge World

To design a physical-industry planning system (**Forge World**), planners can directly operationalize the structural relationships identified in these global frameworks:

1. **The Product Space Topology (Proximity and Relatedness)**: 
   Forge World can model industrial capabilities as a network graph where nodes represent physical products/components and edges represent the probability that a country exports both (calculated via revealed comparative advantage). The **minimum path length** and **density** around a node determine how difficult it is for a regional industrial cluster to pivot toward advanced manufacturing (e.g., transitioning from basic steel fabrication to aerospace turbine manufacturing).
2. **Value-Added Multipliers and Inter-Industry Dependencies**: 
   Using Inter-Country Input-Output (ICIO) matrices from OECD TiVA, Forge World can trace downstream and upstream ripple effects of raw material shocks, bottleneck components, or capacity constraints across international supply chains.
3. **Tacit Capability Vector Space (WIPO Analogue)**: 
   By tracking multi-domain assets (patents, specialized machinery trade, scientific publications, and workforce skills), Forge World can maintain a real-time **Capability Vector** for every node in the industrial network, preventing the hallucination of manufacturing capabilities in regions lacking foundational preconditions.
4. **Vulnerability and Bottleneck Indices (CRMs / SPOFs)**: 
   Adapting the European Critical Raw Materials methodology, Forge World can continuously compute Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) concentration metrics for input materials to identify Single Points of Failure before physical shortages disrupt production.

## Gaps and Unknowns

* **Informal and Shadow Manufacturing**: Most formal databases (UNIDO, OECD, World Bank) capture registered enterprises and official trade flows. The informal manufacturing sector, military-industrial black markets, and dual-use gray-market components are largely unmeasured or categorized as **UNKNOWN**.
* **Real-Time Latency**: Macroeconomic and trade datasets (TiVA, UNIDO CIP, National Input-Output tables) typically operate with a 1-to-3-year reporting lag, making them insufficient for real-time crisis planning without predictive "nowcasting" models.
* **Tacit Knowledge Quantification**: While patent and export data serve as proxies for technological capability, the tacit engineering knowledge required to operate complex physical machinery (e.g., semiconductor lithography or advanced metallurgy) cannot be fully captured by quantitative indicators (**UNCERTAIN CLAIM**).
