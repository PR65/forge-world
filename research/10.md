# Stream Research Note: Existing Products and Research in Industrial Planning, Digital Twins, and Geoeconomic Models

## Scope

This research note investigates the landscape of existing products, academic literature, and institutional frameworks across thirteen core domains: supply-chain digital twins, industrial planning, manufacturing graphs, product-to-supply-chain engines, economic complexity, critical-mineral simulators, strategic supply-chain tools, manufacturing copilots, industrial-policy simulation, factory AI, national digital twins, scenario simulators, and geoeconomic models. The primary objective is to evaluate these systems through a strategic **Copy, Learn, Integrate, and Avoid** lens to inform the architecture of a next-generation physical-industry planning system (**Forge World**). 

The scope encompasses enterprise-grade operational platforms (e.g., Coupa, Relex, Pelico, Palantir Foundry), national and international research initiatives (e.g., Harvard Growth Lab's Atlas of Economic Complexity, National Renewable Energy Laboratory's [NREL] LIBRA and RING models, National Institute of Standards and Technology [NIST] digital twin economic studies), and advanced knowledge graph architectures (AWS manufacturing digital threads and enterprise supply chain graphs). The analysis emphasizes primary data sources, structured extraction, quantitative rigor, and explicit epistemic confidence categorization across all evaluated entities.

---

## Executive Findings

### 1. Enterprise Supply Chain Digital Twins and Factory AI
Enterprise digital twins have evolved from static CAD/ERP reporting dashboards into dynamic simulation engines that ingest real-time IoT and enterprise resource planning (ERP) feeds to model facility bottlenecks and multi-tier disruptions. Commercial platforms such as Coupa, Relex, and Pelico provide granular factory-level scheduling optimization and disruption re-allocation. However, their primary limitation is a restricted operational horizon: they operate inside closed enterprise boundaries or single-tier supplier networks, lacking macroeconomic context or cross-sectoral spillover dynamics. For Forge World, enterprise twins offer valuable algorithmic patterns for real-time rescheduling and inventory buffering (**Learn**), but proprietary SaaS implementations must be bypassed in favor of open, programmable simulation cores (**Avoid**).

### 2. Manufacturing Knowledge Graphs and Product-to-Supply-Chain Engines
Recent developments in manufacturing intelligence emphasize digital threads constructed via knowledge graphs. Platforms deployed on AWS and enterprise knowledge architectures connect Bill of Materials (BOM) structures, machining processes, tier-1 through tier-N suppliers, and logistical transit nodes into unified semantic graphs. These graphs resolve a fundamental information gap by linking specific product designs directly to upstream raw material dependencies. Integrating these semantic graph structures into Forge World is essential for tracing macro-level mineral shortages down to specific factory component specifications (**Integrate**).

### 3. Economic Complexity and National Mineral Simulators
At the macroeconomic and geoeconomic level, the Harvard Growth Lab's **Atlas of Economic Complexity** and the **Observatory of Economic Complexity (OEC)** provide empirical foundations for measuring a society's productive knowledge, export diversification, and product space connectivity. Concurrently, specialized tools like NREL's **Lithium-Ion Battery Resource Assessment (LIBRA)** model and **Recursive Integrated Networks for Growth (RING)** model simulate global critical-mineral supply chains, material flows, and refining bottlenecks under various policy scenarios. These models bridge the gap between macroeconomic capability and physical resource constraints, representing critical reference architectures for Forge World's national and regional planning modules (**Copy/Integrate**).

### 4. Strategic Synthesis: Copy, Learn, Integrate, Avoid
To operationalize these findings for Forge World, the system architecture must adopt a tiered design:
* **Copy**: Mathematical formulations of economic complexity (Economic Complexity Index [ECI] and Product Complexity Index [PCI]) and system dynamics frameworks for critical mineral resource depletion (NREL RING/LIBRA models).
* **Learn**: Algorithmic approaches to real-time shop-floor rescheduling and bottleneck identification from enterprise digital twins (Pelico, NIST economic decision frameworks).
* **Integrate**: Semantic manufacturing knowledge graphs (AWS Digital Thread architectures) linking micro-level BOM components to macro-level supply chains and trade networks.
* **Avoid**: Siloed, proprietary SaaS architectures, black-box vendor pricing models, and standalone optimization tools that lack cross-domain interoperability.

## Source and Dataset Inventory

1. **The Atlas of Economic Complexity (Harvard Growth Lab)**
   * **Description**: Comprehensive international trade dataset covering over 6,000 products across 130+ countries, measuring economic complexity, export sophistication, and growth projections.
   * **Access & APIs**: Web interface, bulk CSV downloads, and programmatic data access via Harvard Growth Lab repositories.
   * **Temporal/Geographic**: Global scope, historical time series from 1995 to the present.
   * **Licensing**: Open data under open-access research terms.
   * **Limitations**: Excludes non-traded services, intellectual property flows, and informal manufacturing economies.

2. **The Observatory of Economic Complexity (OEC)**
   * **Description**: Interactive visualization and data platform tracking international trade, subnational economic profiles, and product network connectivity.
   * **Access & APIs**: RESTful APIs, Python client libraries, and bulk data exports.
   * **Temporal/Geographic**: Global coverage spanning 5,000+ products and subnational administrative units.
   * **Licensing**: Open access with attribution requirements.
   * **Limitations**: Dependent on UN Comtrade reporting quality and timely customs filings by member states.

3. **NREL LIBRA and RING Models (US Department of Energy)**
   * **Description**: System dynamics and supply chain simulation models tracking critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths) from extraction and refining to battery manufacturing and end-of-life recycling.
   * **Access & APIs**: Technical reports, open-source model repositories, and DOE research publications.
   * **Temporal/Geographic**: Global supply chain projections through 2050.
   * **Licensing**: Public domain (U.S. Federal Government research).
   * **Limitations**: Complex system dynamics models require calibration against volatile geopolitical trade policies and rapidly evolving extraction technologies.

4. **NIST Digital Twin Economic Impact Reports**
   * **Description**: Empirical and analytical studies evaluating the return on investment, capital expenditures, and operational efficiency gains of digital twin adoption in discrete manufacturing.
   * **Access & APIs**: Published PDF reports via the NIST technical publication library.
   * **Temporal/Geographic**: United States industrial sector benchmarks (published late 2024).
   * **Licensing**: Public domain.
   * **Limitations**: Focuses primarily on large-scale discrete manufacturing, with less visibility into process industries or small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs).

## Relationships and Variables Useful for Forge World

To construct an effective physical-industry planning engine, Forge World must ingest and operationalize the following core mathematical and relational variables:

* **Economic Complexity Index ($ECI$) and Product Complexity Index ($PCI$)**: Derived from the bipartite country-product matrix $M_{cp}$ via eigenvector methods, capturing the diversity of a country's export basket and the ubiquity of its products. Useful for scoring regional manufacturing capabilities.
* **Node-Edge Connectivity in Manufacturing Knowledge Graphs**: Directed acyclic graphs ($G = (V, E)$) where vertices $V$ represent raw materials, components, manufacturing processes, and facilities, and edges $E$ represent transport logistics, bill-of-material transformations, and capacity constraints.
* **Bottleneck Sensitivity and Lead-Time Multipliers**: Dynamic flow variables tracking inventory buffers, queue times, and single-source dependency ratios across tier-1 through tier-N suppliers.
* **Mineral Extraction-to-Refining Yield Ratios**: Mass-balance coefficients tracking raw mineral throughput (e.g., spodumene ore to battery-grade lithium carbonate) under varying geopolitical trade restrictions and energy cost scenarios.

## Gaps and Unknowns

1. **Macro-Micro Integration Gap**: Current tools maintain a strict dichotomy. Macro models (Atlas, OEC, NREL RING) evaluate country-level trade and mineral flows but lack factory-level BOM resolution. Micro models (Pelico, Coupa, enterprise digital twins) optimize individual shop floors but remain blind to national industrial policy shifts and macroeconomic resource constraints. Forge World must bridge this gap.
2. **Proprietary Data Silos in Tier-N Supply Chains**: Detailed supplier dependency networks and proprietary manufacturing know-how remain locked inside commercial supply chain visibility platforms (Resilinc, Everstream, Palantir). Open-source proxies are often outdated or excessively aggregated.
3. **Dynamic Geopolitical Shock Modeling**: Existing scenario simulators struggle to model non-linear regulatory interventions, export controls, and sudden trade embargoes without relying on static historical elasticities.
