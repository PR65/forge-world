# Verified Swarajya observations (browser, 22 Aug 2026)

## Article
Vedika Znwar, “How India Is Confronting Its Vulnerability In Critical Mineral Supply Chains,” Swarajya Economy, 18 Dec 2025:
https://swarajyamag.com/economy/how-india-is-confronting-its-vulnerability-in-critical-mineral-supply-chains

The article states that India is fully dependent on external suppliers for several critical minerals and imports 60–80% of others; it says China controls nearly 90% of global rare-earth processing. It describes China’s 4 Apr 2025 dual-use export controls covering seven medium/heavy rare-earth elements and related magnets, followed by additional items in October 2025. It reports that the National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM) has a ₹16,300 crore public expenditure component plus ₹18,000 crore of expected state-enterprise/private investment, for an approximate ₹34,300 crore total between 2024 and 2031, with 1,200 exploration projects and processing/recycling measures.

The article also reports a ₹1,500 crore recycling incentive programme with a target of recovering up to 40,000 tonnes of critical minerals from secondary sources; it describes KABIL partnerships involving Argentina, Australia, and Chile; and it reports a ₹7,280 crore programme for domestic rare-earth permanent-magnet production with planned capacity of 6,000 tonnes per year. It states that India imported approximately 54,000 tonnes of rare-earth permanent magnets in 2024–25.

## Evidence status
These are FACTS about what the Swarajya article says, not automatic validation of the underlying numbers. The article is a secondary source and should be paired with official Ministry of Mines, Cabinet, Chinese export-control, KABIL, and Ministry of Finance/industry documents before being used as a model parameter. The article’s strongest Forge World contribution is problem framing: reserves are not enough; midstream processing, purity, recycling, skilled personnel, and overseas supply diversification are separate capability nodes.

The article uses strategic and normative language such as “hegemonic global supply chains” and “economic and strategic priority.” Such framing should be preserved as the publication’s perspective but not confused with neutral measurement. For the final report, claims based only on this article should be labeled MEDIA-REPORTED or UNCERTAIN where primary corroboration is not available.
