# FORGE India V1 — known limitations

FORGE India V1 is a broad industrial buildability atlas. It is designed to answer first-order questions about what a physical product is, what major industrial capabilities it requires, India's broad position, likely gaps and imports, a sensible localization ladder, and the capabilities it depends on or unlocks.

It is not a proof that India can supply a product at a particular specification, quantity, price, quality, or date.

## Read status labels carefully

- `STRONG`, `AVAILABLE`, `PARTIAL`, `SCALE_LIMITED`, `IMPORT_DEPENDENT`, `MISSING`, `UNKNOWN`, and `CONFLICTED` are broad national navigation labels.
- Each page shows an epistemic class. `ASSUMPTION`, `MODELLED`, and `UNKNOWN` labels are discovery priors, not verified supply claims.
- Factory existence is not qualified capability. Trade is not domestic production. Eligibility is not available supply. Nameplate is not effective capacity.
- A status can vary by specification, qualification regime, customer, facility, scale, and date. V1 does not resolve every such variant.

## What V1 does not yet provide

- a complete supplier, company, or facility directory;
- verified allocatable factory capacity, utilization, yield, or delivery dates;
- supplier qualification or RFQ-ready sourcing decisions;
- complete engineering specifications, tolerances, process recipes, or exhaustive BOMs;
- detailed capital cost, project finance, or production economics;
- continuously refreshed evidence for every atlas record;
- a complete row-by-row mirror of IndiaBUILD or any other external catalogue.

## How to use it

Use V1 to orient a build or localization decision, discover upstream requirements, identify likely investigation priorities, and structure the first conversation with engineers, suppliers, laboratories, or investors. Before committing capital or making a procurement claim, validate the relevant specification, evidence, supplier, qualification route, effective capacity, and current commercial terms.

`UNKNOWN` is a valid answer. Where the atlas does not have enough evidence, it should remain visible rather than being converted into false certainty.
