# FORGE INDIA — CANONICAL OPERATING VISION

**Status:** Current canonical operating vision for the India-first phase of FORGE WORLD  
**Decision date:** 2026-08-25  
**Supersedes for current execution:** `docs/FORGE_INDIA_OPERATING_VISION.md` as the more complete operating statement  
**Does not replace:** `docs/FORGE_WORLD_NORTH_STAR.md`

---

## 0. One-sentence definition

> **FORGE INDIA is an evidence-backed industrial buildability compiler and transformation optimizer that takes any physically meaningful objective, decomposes the complete industrial system required to achieve it, determines what India can actually do today, identifies what is missing or uncertain, and compares the fastest, cheapest, most resilient and most sovereign combinations of importing, qualifying, partnering, licensing, joint venturing, acquiring, building, financing, substituting, redesigning and inventing needed to make the objective real.**

The public question is simple:

> # **Can India build this?**

Everything underneath may be extremely deep.

---

## 1. The India-first decision

FORGE WORLD remains the long-term universal ambition. The present operating scope is intentionally narrower and deeper:

- **India is the buildability target.**
- The rest of the world is retained wherever it affects India's ability to build.
- We are not attempting to create a shallow Wikipedia of every country's complete industrial economy before FORGE works deeply for one country.
- No useful existing global research is deleted merely because the operating focus is India.

The current model should therefore be understood as:

```text
DEEP INDIA INDUSTRIAL CORE
        +
SELECTIVE GLOBAL SUPPLY / TECHNOLOGY / CAPITAL-EQUIPMENT / RESOURCE /
LOGISTICS / POLICY / SHOCK / ALTERNATIVE-SOURCING LAYER
```

This is a scope concentration, not a reduction in reasoning ambition.

## 3. The canonical question and answer contract

For an objective such as:

- manufacture an MRI machine in India;
- manufacture 1 million humanoids/year by 2030;
- build 10,000 satellites/year;
- build a reusable rocket engine ecosystem;
- build a nuclear fleet at a specified rate;
- localize a high-end CNC machine;
- make an industrial motor without Chinese rare-earth magnets;
- manufacture a modern passenger aircraft supply chain;
- manufacture agricultural drones at large scale;
- build 100 GW/year of solar equipment;
- localize a pharmaceutical or biotech production system;
- manufacture a food-processing line or textile machine;

FORGE should answer:

1. **What exactly is the objective?** Quantity, date, geography, performance, quality, localization/sovereignty target and allowed sourcing rules.
2. **What valid technical architectures can satisfy it?** There is rarely one canonical BOM.
3. **What does each architecture completely require?** Systems to upstream primitives.
4. **What can India already execute to the required specification, quality, qualification and scale?**
5. **What can India do partially or adjacently?**
6. **What is import-dependent?** From where, under what concentration and restriction risk?
7. **What is genuinely missing?**
8. **What is UNKNOWN or CONFLICTED?**
9. **What transformations can close each gap?**
10. **What sequence or portfolio of actions closes the objective fastest / cheapest / most resiliently / most sovereignly?**
11. **What happens under shocks?**
12. **What evidence supports every consequential conclusion?**

Verdicts remain:

- **FEASIBLE**
- **CONDITIONALLY_FEASIBLE**
- **INFEASIBLE_UNDER_MODEL**
- **NOT_ADJUDICABLE**

No verdict is meaningful without the architecture, time horizon, sourcing rules, assumptions, unresolved unknowns and evidence behind it.

The product may contain hundreds or thousands of decision-relevant requirements. FORGE should retain that depth while using progressive disclosure in the interface.

The question is not merely:

> “Which part does India lack?”

It is first:

> “What does this product actually require?”

Only then:

> “Which of those requirements can India satisfy?”

## 5. Product-first UX, capability-backed engine

The front end should not begin with a catalogue of 10,000 obscure capabilities.

The user starts with the product/objective and receives a plain-language summary:

- Can India build it?
- What share is strong domestic capability?
- What share is partial / scale-limited / specification-limited?
- What share is import-dependent?
- What is missing?
- What is UNKNOWN?
- Which 5–20 bottlenecks actually decide the result?
- Why does each bottleneck matter?
- What are the best localization / sourcing / redesign paths?
- How long might closure take under explicit assumptions?

Experts can then drill down:

```text
MRI machine
→ magnet system
→ superconducting coil
→ conductor specification
→ material purity
→ winding process
→ heat treatment
→ machine/tooling
→ metrology
→ qualification
→ Indian candidate facility
→ capacity evidence
→ foreign dependency
→ source
```

A spring, seal, bearing, valve or coating should be presented in the context of the parent system and failure mode it enables.

## 8. Industrial transformation is broader than localization

This was under-specified in the first India draft. The action space must be explicit.

When a requirement is unavailable or insufficient in India, FORGE should be able to compare combinations of:

### Source / secure
- **BUY / IMPORT**
- **DIVERSIFY IMPORT SOURCES**
- **LONG-TERM SUPPLY / OFFTAKE CONTRACT**
- **STRATEGIC INVENTORY / STOCKPILE** where physically and economically appropriate
- **ALTERNATE LOGISTICS / ROUTE**

### Partner / obtain technology
- **CONTRACT MANUFACTURE**
- **LICENSE TECHNOLOGY**
- **TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER**
- **JOINT VENTURE (JV)**
- **JOINT DEVELOPMENT / CO-DEVELOPMENT**
- **ACQUIRE A COMPANY / TECHNOLOGY / IP / PRODUCT LINE** where legally and practically possible

### Build / scale in India
- **GREENFIELD BUILD**
- **BROWNFIELD EXPANSION**
- **DOMESTIC EQUIPMENT / TOOLING BUILD**
- **IMPORT SEED EQUIPMENT THEN LOCALIZE THE TOOLCHAIN**
- **SUPPLIER DEVELOPMENT**
- **QUALIFY AN EXISTING INDIAN SUPPLIER**
- **DUAL-SOURCE / SECOND-SOURCE QUALIFICATION**
- **BUILD TEST / METROLOGY / CERTIFICATION INFRASTRUCTURE**
- **BUILD WORKFORCE / TACIT-KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER PIPELINE**

### Finance / enable
- **DIRECT PRIVATE CAPEX**
- **DEBT / PROJECT FINANCE**
- **PUBLIC OR STRATEGIC FUNDING**
- **GRANT / R&D SUPPORT**
- **PROCUREMENT OR OFFTAKE GUARANTEE**
- **PPP / CONSORTIUM** where appropriate
- **TARGETED POLICY INCENTIVE / FAST-TRACK INFRASTRUCTURE OR PERMITTING** where the objective warrants it

### Change the technical problem
- **SUBSTITUTE MATERIAL**
- **SUBSTITUTE COMPONENT**
- **SUBSTITUTE PROCESS**
- **USE AN ADJACENT INDIAN CAPABILITY**
- **RELAX / CHANGE A NON-ESSENTIAL SPECIFICATION** if the objective still remains valid
- **REDESIGN THE PARENT ARCHITECTURE**
- **RECYCLE / RECOVER / REUSE** as a real secondary-production route where technically valid
- **DEVELOP AN INDIGENOUS R&D / INVENTION ROUTE**

### Research before acting
- **QUALIFY THE UNKNOWN**
- **RUN SUPPLIER AUDIT / FIRST-ARTICLE / PILOT**
- **GENERATE TARGETED RESEARCH TASK**

This action taxonomy is not a menu of slogans. Each candidate pathway should alter the graph and be re-solved.

For example, a JV is useful only if its actual effect is modeled: technology access, equipment, process know-how, IP/control, local content, facility ramp, qualification, capacity, cost, time and residual foreign dependency.

The system should compare pathways across:

- time to usable qualified output;
- CapEx / recurring cost where evidence allows;
- technical risk;
- qualification risk;
- implementation risk;
- scale potential;
- IP/control and sovereignty;
- residual import dependence;
- resilience;
- downstream option value.

## 9. Industrial time: money does not create capacity instantly

FORGE must model the state transitions between intention and usable production.

Typical project states include:

```text
CONCEPT
→ ANNOUNCED
→ FUNDED / FINANCED
→ PERMITTED / SITE-READY where relevant
→ UNDER CONSTRUCTION
→ EQUIPMENT INSTALLED
→ COMMISSIONED
→ PILOT / INITIAL OUTPUT
→ RAMPING
→ PROCESS-STABLE
→ QUALIFIED
→ MATURE / REPEATABLE CAPACITY
```

Lead times may include:

- facility design/construction;
- long-lead capital equipment;
- grid/water/utility connection;
- tooling development;
- commissioning;
- yield ramp;
- workforce learning;
- supplier onboarding;
- regulatory/customer qualification;
- material/process requalification;
- logistics setup.

A project announcement is not future capacity until the relevant state and uncertainty are represented.

## 10. Yield, purity, quality and usable output are first-class

Several old FORGE research conclusions are important enough to be constitutional for India.

### Purity / grade ladders

“India produces this chemical/material” is too coarse.

Commodity grade, industrial grade, battery grade, electronic grade, semiconductor grade, aerospace-qualified grade, nuclear grade or other process-qualified states may be different capabilities with different equipment, contamination control, packaging, analytics and qualification.

### Yield is an output multiplier

Nameplate capacity is not usable capacity.

FORGE should distinguish, where evidence allows:

```text
nameplate
× availability / uptime
× yield
× specification acceptance
× qualification eligibility
× allocatable share
= usable capacity for the objective
```

Scrap, rework, test failure and ramp loss can create additional upstream demand and time.

---

## 11. Recursive industrial closure: machines build machines

An advanced product depends on factories; factories depend on equipment; equipment depends on deeper industries.

FORGE must explicitly support recursion such as:

```text
humanoid
→ precision reducer
→ precision gear
→ gear grinding
→ CNC grinder
→ spindle / bearings / drives / controls / metrology
→ their own process and material chains
```

This is often cyclic, not a clean tree.

FORGE should therefore reason about:

- strongly connected industrial dependencies;
- bootstrap sets;
- imported seed equipment;
- minimum viable industrial closure;
- progressively localized toolchains;
- which foundational capability additions unlock many downstream products.

The goal is not infinite recursion. Recurse deepest where the node is bottlenecked, concentrated, sovereignty-sensitive, technically difficult, qualification-heavy, long-lead or high option-value.

---

## 12. Supplier state and qualification must remain explicit

A named manufacturer is not automatically a usable supplier.

Canonical progression:

```text
KNOWN MANUFACTURER
→ POTENTIAL SUPPLIER
→ TECHNICALLY CAPABLE CANDIDATE
→ SAMPLE / PROTOTYPE / FIRST ARTICLE
→ PROCESS / QUALITY QUALIFIED
→ CUSTOMER / REGULATORY QUALIFIED
→ PRODUCTION-RATE QUALIFIED
→ AVAILABLE ALLOCATABLE CAPACITY
```

Switching suppliers may require tooling transfer, PPAP/FAI/FAIR, reliability testing, material/process requalification, special-process accreditation, software/firmware validation, documentation, customer approval and ramp.

FORGE EXECUTE should surface these gates rather than recommend “use supplier X” because a website lists a vaguely similar product.

---

## 13. Competing demand prevents fictional capacity

India's objective cannot simply seize all copper, wafer capacity, machine-tool hours or skilled engineers because the solver wants them.

FORGE should model competition for shared resources when decision-relevant, including:

- minerals and refined materials;
- high-purity chemicals and industrial gases;
- semiconductor wafer / packaging capacity by technology class;
- machine-tool hours;
- specialized capital equipment;
- metrology and test facilities;
- freight corridors / ports;
- grid connections / electricity;
- water and industrial utilities;
- qualified engineering labor;
- scarce imported components.

The target plan receives only the share plausibly available under the modeled scenario.

---

## 14. Inventory, buffers and recovery matter under shocks

A shock is not an instantaneous percentage haircut.

Where data support it, resilience should distinguish:

```text
shock onset
→ inventory / strategic-buffer drawdown
→ supplier / logistics adaptation
→ rationing / allocation
→ alternate-source qualification
→ substitution / redesign
→ new capacity ramp
→ recovery
```

Strategic inventory can therefore be a valid bridge action while localization or substitution ramps, but it is not equivalent to sovereign production capability.

## 16. The world remains India's external industrial environment

No useful global research should be thrown away.

Foreign data remains relevant when it can answer:

- Who can supply the missing item now?
- Where are the actual facilities and clusters?
- What is the known/unknown capacity?
- What countries control upstream materials or processing?
- Which machine tools or production systems must India import first?
- Who owns the relevant IP or process know-how?
- Where could a JV, licence, acquisition or technology-transfer partnership come from?
- How concentrated is the dependency?
- What alternative sources exist?
- What export controls, sanctions or legal restrictions apply?
- What shipping corridor connects supply to India?
- What foreign demand competes for the same supply?
- What happens if a country or corridor becomes unavailable?

High-value foreign geographies include, as relevant to the dependency being solved, China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Germany/Central Europe, the United States, Russia, Australia and other resource/industrial economies.

FORGE should not claim exhaustive country models for them unless separately built and evidenced.

## 17. Shocks and resilience remain first-class

The baseline question is:

> **Can India build it?**

The resilience question is:

> **Can India still build it when the world becomes hostile, expensive or disrupted?**

Shock families may include:

- war / geopolitical conflict;
- sanctions;
- export controls;
- tariffs and trade restrictions;
- rare-earth / graphite / mineral restrictions;
- semiconductor shortages;
- oil-price shock;
- natural-gas / electricity-price shock;
- copper, nickel, lithium or other material-price shocks;
- freight-rate spike;
- shipping-route closure;
- port outage;
- currency depreciation;
- interest-rate / financing shock where material;
- electricity shortage;
- drought / water constraint where relevant;
- natural disaster;
- mine/refinery/factory outage;
- loss of a critical capital-equipment supplier;
- sudden competing demand;
- delayed project;
- failed qualification;
- beneficial shocks such as a new mine, refinery, technology, policy or domestic project.

A shock should modify real constraints and trigger a re-solve.

FORGE should show:

- which products/objectives fail first;
- which dependency caused the failure;
- time until buffer exhaustion where known;
- available alternative suppliers/routes;
- qualification delay;
- substitute/redesign paths;
- localization actions with best resilience gain.

Legal or ethical sourcing constraints, including sanctions, forced-labor-linked import bans or similar rules, should enter only when they create an actual eligibility/sourcing constraint; FORGE does not need a generic ESG bureaucracy layer.

## 18. Policy, finance and ownership are part of real industrial transformation

Some industrial gaps exist because physics is hard. Others exist because the economics, demand certainty, financing, policy or ownership structure does not support investment.

FORGE should therefore distinguish:

- technically impossible under current knowledge;
- technically possible but no qualified Indian producer;
- producer exists but lacks capital;
- capital exists but demand/offtake is insufficient;
- equipment or technology right is unavailable;
- project is blocked by infrastructure/permitting;
- project is feasible through JV/licence/technology transfer faster than indigenous development;
- strategic sovereignty objective justifies a higher-cost domestic pathway.

Where supported, DESIGN/EXECUTE should compare relevant interventions such as:

- private investment;
- consortium/JV;
- strategic acquisition;
- government procurement/offtake;
- targeted incentives;
- grants / R&D support;
- infrastructure connection;
- supplier-development programme;
- standards/test infrastructure;
- local-content / qualification pathway;
- strategic stockpile;
- technology-right acquisition.

The objective is not to become an industrial-policy bureaucracy simulator. Add policy/finance only when it materially changes whether and when a physical capability becomes real.

---

## 19. Cost, sovereignty, resilience and speed are different objectives

There is rarely one universally “best” industrial plan.

FORGE should be able to show trade-offs between:

- fastest path;
- lowest expected cost;
- highest domestic value addition;
- highest domestic design/IP ownership;
- lowest single-country dependency;
- highest shock resilience;
- highest probability of qualification by deadline;
- highest cross-sector option value.

For example:

- **Import now** may be fastest.
- **JV + technology transfer** may create capability fastest with partial external dependence.
- **Acquisition** may buy time but have legal/control constraints.
- **Greenfield indigenous development** may maximize sovereignty but miss the deadline.
- **Architecture redesign** may eliminate the dependency entirely at a performance/cost trade-off.

The user should be able to understand why FORGE recommends one path under one priority and another under a different priority.

## 20. DISCOVER: find capabilities worth building even before a single product asks for them

FORGE should not only answer individual objective questions.

It should identify shared foundational bottlenecks and **industrial option value**:

> Which one capability, facility, test system, machine, material process or supplier-development intervention unlocks the largest number of important downstream objectives?

Examples include:

- high-end machine tools;
- precision grinding;
- metrology;
- large forgings;
- vacuum heat treatment;
- high-purity chemistry;
- power electronics;
- industrial controls;
- specialty bearings;
- advanced welding/NDT;
- servo systems;
- semiconductor process equipment;
- industrial gases;
- qualified test infrastructure.

This is how FORGE can move from “Can India build X?” to “What should India build next to unlock many Xs?”

## 21. Reverse buildability and minimum intervention remain core

FORGE should also answer inverse questions:

- Given India's current capability and capacity, **how many units could India build now?**
- At 30%, 50%, 70%, 90% domestic-value/localization targets, what output is possible?
- What is the smallest set of interventions required to cross the feasibility threshold?
- Which unknown, if resolved, is most likely to change the verdict?
- Which capability investment removes the most strategic dependencies across many sectors?

This preserves the original transformation-optimizer character of FORGE rather than turning it into an encyclopedic report generator.

## 22. Evidence and epistemic integrity are constitutional

Canonical classes:

- **MEASURED**
- **REPORTED**
- **DERIVED**
- **ESTIMATED**
- **MODELLED**
- **ASSUMPTION**
- **SCENARIO**
- **UNKNOWN**
- **CONFLICTED**

Hard rules:

- UNKNOWN != zero
- negative search != proof of absence
- import eligibility != physical supply
- trade != production capability
- company existence != qualified supplier
- facility existence != qualified capability
- installed equipment != production readiness
- nameplate != effective capacity
- effective capacity != allocatable capacity
- project announcement != operating capacity
- patent != production capability
- mineral reserve != usable industrial material
- recipe existence != qualified throughput
- potential supplier != qualified supplier
- precise numbers require evidence, transparent derivation or explicit assumption
- LLM output is never numerical authority
- substitutions/redesigns require technical validity and re-solve
- stale evidence remains historical and receives a freshness/recheck state rather than being silently overwritten

Every consequential public conclusion should be traceable:

```text
verdict
→ assumption / constraint
→ bottleneck
→ requirement / dependency
→ organization / facility / resource
→ claim
→ evidence
→ source
```

The model should prefer an honest NOT_ADJUDICABLE answer over manufactured precision.

## 23. Research and value of information

FORGE should turn important ignorance into useful work.

For every decision-critical UNKNOWN, record:

- exact missing fact;
- why it changes the objective;
- likely source class;
- recommended research query / supplier audit / test / quotation / site check;
- estimated decision value where possible.

Research should be prioritized by **value of information**, not merely by how incomplete the graph looks.

A missing low-impact bolt capacity number should not outrank an unknown high-field magnet qualification constraint that can flip an MRI verdict.

## 26. Canonical product modes

### ANALYZE

Answer whether India can achieve an objective under explicit architecture, capacity, time, sourcing and policy constraints. Surface WHY, bottlenecks and UNKNOWNs.

### DESIGN

Generate/compare technically valid architectures, substitutions and graph rewrites. Re-solve every candidate; do not assume substitutes are free.

### EXECUTE

Convert the chosen path into work packages: BUY / QUALIFY / LICENSE / JV / ACQUIRE / BUILD / FUND / DEVELOP-SUPPLIER / BUILD-INFRASTRUCTURE / SUBSTITUTE / REDESIGN / RESEARCH, with sequencing, dependencies, qualification and ramp gates.

### DISCOVER

Identify shared bottlenecks, strategic vulnerabilities, high-option-value capabilities, cluster/facility opportunities and interventions that unlock many downstream products.

### LEARN

Explain the industrial system progressively from product-level intuition to exact engineering/manufacturing primitives and evidence.

## 27. Recommended public product experience

A public analysis should initially show:

### 1. Objective

**Can India build [X] at [quantity] by [date] under [localization/sourcing/performance constraints]?**

### 2. Verdict

FEASIBLE / CONDITIONALLY_FEASIBLE / INFEASIBLE_UNDER_MODEL / NOT_ADJUDICABLE.

### 3. India buildability composition

- strong domestic;
- partial/scale-limited;
- specification gap;
- qualification gap;
- import-dependent;
- missing;
- unknown/conflicted.

### 4. Complete architecture tree

All major systems/components, not only red nodes.

### 5. Critical bottlenecks

Human-readable explanation of why they matter.

### 6. Global dependencies

Countries, facilities, materials, equipment, licensors, corridors, concentration.

### 7. Transformation alternatives

Import / diversify / stockpile / qualify / supplier-development / license / technology-transfer / JV / acquisition / brownfield / greenfield / finance / substitute / redesign / R&D.

### 8. Time / critical path

When each action becomes usable qualified capability.

### 9. Geography

Relevant Indian clusters/facilities/infrastructure and foreign sources/routes.

### 10. Shocks

Re-run under selected disruptions.

### 11. WHY / evidence

Every important conclusion drillable to source.

### 12. Research queue

Decision-critical unknowns ranked by value of information.

---

## 28. What not to build

FORGE India should **not** become:

- a flat India capability wiki;
- an IndiaBUILD clone;
- a supplier directory;
- a generic supply-chain dashboard;
- a giant graph visualization with no solver meaning;
- a political-score dashboard;
- a giant spreadsheet of country rankings;
- an LLM-generated industrial encyclopedia;
- a fake-precision simulator;
- a static BOM viewer;
- an endless research/audit project.

The India-first scope exists specifically to avoid building a shallow global encyclopedia.

---

## 29. What “public-ready FORGE India V1” means

V1 does not mean omniscient knowledge of every workshop, machine or screw in India.

A strong public V1 means:

- objective-first UX works;
- the 12-domain map is represented;
- IndiaBUILD breadth is accounted for or explicitly unresolved;
- foundational industrial primitives are reusable across sectors;
- diverse objectives can compose substantial dependency trees without bespoke manual packs for every query;
- complete product decomposition is visible;
- India status is evidence-bounded;
- global dependencies are retained selectively;
- transformation actions include JV/acquisition/licensing/supplier development/finance as well as build/import/substitute/redesign;
- time/ramp/qualification are modeled;
- yield/purity/capacity distinctions are preserved;
- recursive machine/tool dependencies exist where strategically important;
- shocks can be re-solved;
- UNKNOWNs are visible and generate research tasks;
- proof/evidence is inspectable;
- the system has been tested on a deliberately diverse India benchmark battery;
- it is useful enough that a serious user can learn something non-obvious and act on it.

Public V1 is a strong, evidence-backed industrial reasoning instrument — not a claim to complete knowledge of India's economy.

## 30. Current execution priority

For the current major India campaign, spend effort in this order:

1. preserve correctness/evidence contracts;
2. ingest and reconcile the existing research corpus once;
3. establish IndiaBUILD parity/accounting;
4. expand all-sector India capability breadth;
5. deepen shared foundational primitives;
6. add Indian organizations/facilities/geography where decision-relevant;
7. add global suppliers/resources/equipment/technology only where they connect to Indian dependency or transformation paths;
8. implement/strengthen complete objective decomposition and reusable composition;
9. ensure transformation actions include the full BUILD / BUY / QUALIFY / LICENSE / JV / ACQUIRE / FUND / SUBSTITUTE / REDESIGN / RESEARCH family;
10. connect project time, qualification, yield and effective capacity;
11. exercise shocks and resilience;
12. make the public UX objective-first and progressively disclosed;
13. run a deliberately diverse India benchmark battery;
14. close only decision-critical gaps;
15. perform one bounded final validation and prepare for deployment.

Do not burn the budget on repeated audits, decorative global coverage, duplicated corpus reads or an exhaustive world encyclopedia.
